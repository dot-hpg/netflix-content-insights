      Business insights

      Netflix is pivoting to TVTV's share of additions rose from 25% in 2018 to 34% in 2021, and TV now outnumbers movies among the newest releases.
      Volume has plateauedAdditions peaked at 2,016 in 2019. Growth now has to come from better targeting, not more titles.
      The library is adult-firstKids/family and reality are the fastest-growing genres, a gap Netflix is already filling.
      International content is the coreEach market has a distinct format and genre personality, from Indian movies to Korean TV.
      Heavy experimentationTwo in three shows end after one season.
      Timing is deliberateFridays, the 1st of the month, July and December carry the biggest launches.

      Recommendations

      Make more TV seriesEspecially international dramas, crime and kids' shows.
      Renew good showsTwo in three stop after season 1; set clear rules to give finished-and-loved shows a second season.
      Grow family and kids' contentOnly 1 in 15 titles is for little kids, yet it is one of the fastest-growing areas. Use proven animation hubs (India, Canada).
      Go localIndian movies and web-series, anime from Japan, K-dramas from Korea, Spanish-language crime from Mexico and Spain, partners in Nigeria and Egypt.
      Keep movies at 90 to 110 minutesKids' movies can be around an hour.
      Launch big shows on a Friday, in July or DecemberKeep February for smaller titles.
      Invest in growing genresReality, romance, action and kids; spend carefully on stand-up and documentaries.
      Co-produce to enter new countriesIt lowers cost and risk.
      Add classic films selectivelyA cheap way to attract older viewers.
      Track what people watchNot just what is added, and review these bets every quarter.
